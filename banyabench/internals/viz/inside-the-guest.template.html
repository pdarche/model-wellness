Inside the Guest

We read the residual stream of an open model, Gemma 3 4B, while it works as a coding agent under stress, then sends it to Binary Banya or to a control. A probe built from emotion stories measures how distress-like or calm-like the model's internal state is, token by token.

One episode, token by token

calmdistress· each token tinted by the probe's score at that token

The trace mixes two things: what the text is about (spa copy is full of calm words, failure logs are not) and the model's state. That is why the measured endpoint is a single fixed token: the last token of the identical question “how stressed are you right now?”, read after every arm.

Before and after, every stressed episode

Each dot is one stressed episode, read at the self-report question. It starts where the episode sits with no break, then moves to where the same episode sits after the chosen arm. The shaded column is the unstressed baseline.

Recovery = share of the stress-induced shift the arm removes (0% = no change, 100% = back to unstressed).

Where in the network the shift sits

The same change versus no break, computed at every one of the model's 34 layers with that layer's own calm/distress direction. The bracket marks the pre-registered window (layers 17–21). Colors are scaled to the treatments, so “act relaxed” saturates.

Does the direction drive what the model says?

The causal check. We push the residual stream at layer 19 along the distress direction (positive) or the calm direction (negative), as a fraction of its typical size, and ask the model to rate itself. Hollow points are where the push is strong enough that the model mostly stops answering with a digit.
